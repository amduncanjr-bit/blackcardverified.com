Everything you need to know about HBCU homecoming and nothing that will actually prepare you

You cannot study for this.

There is no orientation packet. There is no itinerary that will hold. The moment you step onto that yard, you are operating on a different clock — one calibrated by the drum major's whistle and the smell of someone's auntie's pound cake appearing from absolutely nowhere. Welcome. You're already late. Keep moving.

The yard is a whole ecosystem and you need to read it fast

The yard during homecoming is not a place you walk through. It is a place you perform, whether you mean to or not. Your outfit is a statement. Your section in the stands is a statement. The specific way you greet somebody you haven't seen since graduation — that long pause before the hug, the handshake that becomes a whole conversation — is a statement. Everyone is saying something at all times and the fluent ones catch every word.

You will see a woman in heels so tall they should be illegal on grass. She will not stumble once. You will see a man in a Greek jacket he has kept in pristine condition for twenty-three years. It still fits. Do not ask how. The alumni who graduated before you had internet access are dressed the best. Accept this and move on.

The bands are why you are here, even if you think you're here for something else. When the drumline locks in, your body responds before your brain does. That's not an accident. That's decades of tradition vibrating in your chest and you just happen to be standing inside it.

By halftime, you will have made three new best friends, eaten something you can't identify but will absolutely request again, and completely lost track of the actual football score.

The social rules are real even though nobody wrote them down

You speak to people. This is non-negotiable. Not a nod — words. Full sentences if the moment calls for it. The yard runs on acknowledgment, and stingy energy gets remembered.

If you are an alumnus, you will spend at least forty-five minutes standing in one spot because everyone who needs to find you will find you there. Do not fight this. The yard has a gravity and it knows where to put people.

If you are a first-timer — brought by a partner, a cousin, a friend who said just trust me — your only job is to stay close, stay curious, and never, under any circumstances, say that another school's homecoming was similar to this. It wasn't. Let it go.

The Greek strolls will stop traffic and they should. The step shows will make you question what your body has been doing with its time. A capella groups will hit a chord that catches you off guard and suddenly you have feelings about a school you didn't even attend. This is normal. This is homecoming working as intended.

What you're actually going to remember

Not the score. Rarely the score. You're going to remember the conversation that started at the tailgate and somehow continued in the parking lot at midnight. You're going to remember the moment the band hit that song — you know the one — and the entire section moved like one body. You're going to remember being somewhere that was built for you, not retrofitted for you, not tolerating you — actually, deliberately, architecturally built for you.

HBCUs have been doing that since the 1800s. Homecoming is just the one weekend a year they let everybody witness it.

Come correct. Eat everything. Hug your people. And if you want to spend the weeks before settling a few debates about Black history, music, and culture while you're hyping yourself up for the yard — Black Card Verified is free on the App Store and it's got an HBCU Life category that will either prepare you or humble you. Either way, you needed it.
