HBCU homecoming: a field guide disguised as a love letter

You will not be ready and you will go anyway.

Nobody's first HBCU homecoming is their best one. Your best one is whenever you finally stop treating it like a concert and start treating it like a reunion, a revival, and a city you only get to live in for four days a year. The game changes. So do you.

But since you're going — and you are going — let's talk about how this actually works, because the brochure is useless and the internet will send you with the wrong shoes.

The schedule is a suggestion and the parking situation is a prophecy

The official homecoming schedule is a document that exists primarily to give you something to photograph and ignore. The step show starts when it starts. The yard fills up when it fills up. The cookout the old heads are throwing three blocks off campus that nobody invited you to but everybody's welcome at? That starts at noon and doesn't end — it just becomes a different party. Your actual itinerary is built in real time, in the group chat, by whoever has the most energy and the least sense. Follow them.

Parking is not a logistics problem. Parking is a spiritual test. You will circle. You will negotiate. You will park somewhere you are not supposed to park and walk farther than you planned in shoes you should not have worn. This happens to everyone. This has always happened to everyone. Factor it in and do not complain out loud because the person next to you just walked from further.

The yard is doing three things at once and all of them are correct

There is a band somewhere. There is a full argument happening between two people in lawn chairs who have not moved in four hours. There is a woman in a fit so constructed it deserves a plaque. There are children running in directions their parents are not tracking. There is somebody's uncle selling something from a cooler. There is a Greek organization doing something that requires the space you are currently standing in, and they will not ask you to move — they will simply begin, and you will move because you understand.

The yard at HBCU homecoming is the only place where everything is happening and somehow none of it is chaos.

What you are actually watching, if you slow down enough to watch it, is continuity in real time. The alumni who graduated in '89 are standing next to the seniors who walk in the spring, and somewhere in that distance is every version of this school that ever existed. That's not sentimental — that's just what's happening. You feel it or you don't, but it's there either way.

You will leave knowing people you did not arrive knowing

This is the part nobody warns you about. You came with your people and you will leave with more of them. Somebody's roommate's cousin. The woman who complimented your jacket and ended up standing next to you for the entire second half of the game. The guy who was at every single event and you don't know his name but you've nodded at him so many times he's basically family now. HBCU homecoming expands your circle without asking permission.

That's the whole thing, really. Every institution has a homecoming. Not every institution has this — the particular feeling of being somewhere built for you, surrounded by people who came back on purpose, celebrating something that took real intention to build and real love to keep. If you want to actually test how deep that love runs — the history, the music, the culture, all of it — Black Card Verified has a whole category for HBCU life and it will humble you in the best way.

Wear comfortable shoes. Charge your phone. Don't make plans you intend to keep.

You'll figure out the rest when you get there. That's always been the point.
