The group chat has bylaws and everyone knows their assignment

Nobody voted and yet here you all are.

The group chat is not casual. You think it's casual because there's no agenda, no dress code, and at least one member who has been on mute since 2021 but still somehow knows everything. Do not be deceived. The group chat is a governing body with a constitution that was never written down and a power structure that was never discussed out loud and yet — everyone in there knows exactly where they stand.

It started, as most institutions do, with a moment of necessity. Somebody's cousin was getting married, or everybody needed to coordinate for the cookout, or one person said we should make a group chat and did it before anyone could object. The name got set — something funny, something that made sense in the moment, something that will now appear as your contact group until one of you dies. Nobody has changed it. Nobody will.

The roles are permanent until further notice

Every functioning group chat has the same core cabinet. The Sender is the one who keeps the chat alive — memes at 7 a.m., prayer requests at noon, drama updates by 9 p.m. This person is doing a public service and deserves recognition they will never receive. The Reactor doesn't type full sentences but their emojis are load-bearing — a well-placed crying-laughing face at the right moment is governance. The Late Responder surfaces six hours after the conversation peaked to say something perfectly correct about a topic nobody is discussing anymore, and somehow that's fine, because we know who they are. The Lurker has the read receipts on and we don't talk about it.

Then there is The Admin. The Admin did not apply. The Admin was simply the person who created the chat, which means they have the power to add and remove members and have used exactly none of it except that one time they added somebody's new boyfriend and then had to quietly remove him after the breakup. The Admin is not a leader. The Admin is a landlord who also lives in the building.

The person who left the chat once and came back is on a different kind of probation that nobody discusses openly.

Succession is a whole thing

Here is where it gets constitutional. What happens when The Sender goes quiet? Maybe life got heavy, maybe they started a new chapter, maybe they finally got a therapist who told them to log off. For a moment the chat flatlines. Two people send memes that don't land. Someone posts a good morning GIF with no follow-through. There is a silence that feels like a waiting room.

And then — somebody rises. It's not announced. There's no vote, no ceremony. One person just starts sending more, starts tagging people, starts asking y'all see this? with the energy of someone who has accepted a calling. The succession has occurred. The old Sender, if they return, finds their position has been maintained in their name, like a throne kept warm. There is no resentment. The chat needed to survive.

What nobody talks about is the group chat that should have died but didn't. It's still technically active. The last real message was in March of some year nobody wants to identify. Every few months someone sends a meme and there are three reactions and then silence again. That chat is a monument now. You don't leave it. You don't revive it. You just let it sit there in your list like a photo you're not ready to put away.

The group chat holds things that wouldn't fit anywhere else — the grief, the hype, the three-minute voice note that nobody asked for and everyone listened to. If you want to test how well a group of people actually knows each other, Black Card Verified has a multiplayer mode that will answer that question faster than you expected. But the group chat already knows. It has always known. That's the whole point.
