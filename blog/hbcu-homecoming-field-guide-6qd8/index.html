HBCU homecoming: a field guide disguised as a love letter

Nothing about this weekend can be explained to someone who wasn't there, and that's the whole point.

You did not attend HBCU homecoming. You survived it, you witnessed it, you were changed by it in ways you are still processing on a random Tuesday in March. People who went to PWIs will say they had a homecoming too. Nod politely. They are describing something else entirely.

Here is what the brochure will never tell you: HBCU homecoming is not an event. It is a season. It begins the moment the first person in the group chat types the name of the school followed by a year and three fire emojis, and it does not end until the last car pulls out of the parking lot of a Waffle House three states away, everyone quiet, everyone full, everyone already thinking about next year.

The itinerary is a suggestion and everyone knows this

On paper, homecoming has a schedule. The step show is at seven. The game kicks off at one. The yard opens at ten. In practice, none of these times mean what they appear to mean. You will arrive when you arrive. The yard will receive you. Somebody's auntie has a lawn chair and a cooler and has been stationed near the gate since the morning, and she will catch you up on everything you missed like a one-woman news broadcast with editorial opinions included at no extra charge.

The step show is its own religion. You already know which organization you're watching for, and you will be impartial about all the others until you are not. The game matters but the stands matter more — who's sitting where, who came back, who brought who, who looks good, who looks like they are thriving and doesn't want to say it directly but wants you to notice, which you will.

The yard on homecoming weekend is the only place where fifteen years can disappear in the time it takes someone to say your government name with that specific inflection that means they knew you when.

What is actually being celebrated here

On the surface, homecoming is football and bands and yard parties and fashion that did not come to play or compromise. But under all of it, something else is happening. HBCUs were built during a time when the country had made its position on Black education very clear, and Black people built these institutions anyway — Spelman in 1881, Howard in 1867, Morehouse in 1867, Fisk in 1866 — and kept building them and funding them and sending their children there and sending those children back to send more children. Homecoming is the reunion of everyone those institutions produced. That's not nothing. That is, in fact, everything.

Which is why the band hits different at an HBCU game. Which is why the fashion is not casual. Which is why people fly in from everywhere, rent cars, book hotels four months in advance, coordinate outfits across group chats with their own pinned messages and their own informal constitutions. You are not just coming back to campus. You are coming back to proof.

What to bring and what to leave home

Bring: comfortable shoes you are willing to sacrifice, cash, a portable charger, patience for traffic, and the understanding that plans will change approximately every forty-five minutes and this is correct.

Leave home: any expectation that this will be efficient, any hope of an early night, and anyone who is going to need a lot of explaining. Homecoming does not have time to onboard anyone. Either you feel it or you watch other people feel it, and only one of those is worth the flight.

The culture has always known how to celebrate itself. Black Card Verified's HBCU Life category exists for the people who want to keep the knowledge sharp between homecoming seasons — free on the App Store, for the culture, by the culture. But the real exam is the weekend itself. You either pass or you schedule a retake for next October.
